# MAN-VIB-001 Vibration Severity and Diagnosis Guide
Applies to: pump, motor, compressor

## 2.1 Severity levels
Overall vibration velocity: below 2.8 mm/s is good; 2.8 to 4.5 mm/s is acceptable; 4.5 to 7.1 mm/s is unsatisfactory and needs inspection (warning level 4.5 mm/s); above 7.1 mm/s is unacceptable and risks damage (alarm level 7.1 mm/s).

## 2.2 Distinguishing common vibration faults
Imbalance appears as a steady vibration at running speed that does not trend. Misalignment shows higher axial vibration. Looseness gives erratic readings. Bearing wear shows a gradual, sustained increase in vibration over days or weeks, often together with a small temperature rise on the same asset. A rising trend combined with a temperature rise therefore points to bearing wear.

## 2.3 Transient spikes
Start-up, load changes and sensor faults produce brief spikes lasting minutes to a few hours, after which readings return to normal. A spike is not a fault unless it is sustained for at least 6 hours or the underlying trend keeps rising. Single-sample extreme readings are almost always sensor errors.
